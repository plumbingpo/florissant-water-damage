Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Florissant

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Moisture enters each board through its underside and edges, the boards swell and press together, and cupping begins as the edges lift above the middle. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Expect it to run a week or more, which is longer than drywall or carpet needs. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

Warning Signs

  • Wide spaces between planks once the wood dries too quickly
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
  • Nail heads and seams ringed by dark staining
  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
  • A musty smell rising from the floor with nothing visible on top
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Stains From Nails and Staples

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Moisture Under the Boards

Long after the boards seem dry, the subfloor may still be holding water. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. Lasting gaps and split or cracked boards are the usual outcome.

How We Fix It

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

Boards swell and the edges rise. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.
