Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Florissant: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. Our team in Florissant finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Damaged Flooring

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Coverage

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • The type of flooring and finishes involved, since some dry in place and others do not.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
  • The number of rooms and floors affected, such as a ceiling underneath the leaking line.
  • Any mold found, since it must be contained and removed before the drying can be completed.

What We Do

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Final Treatment and Records

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.
